Keyword density, explained

What is keyword density, and what's a good number?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears in a text, as a share of the total word count. Use "cold brew" 10 times in a 1,000-word article and its density is 1%.

Google has never published an ideal percentage, and density isn't a ranking factor on its own. It's still a useful check for two real problems. One is a page that never clearly names its topic. The other is a page that repeats a phrase so often it reads as keyword stuffing, which Google's spam policies call out.

Open method

How we count, so you can trust the number

Different checkers disagree because they count differently. Here are our rules, written down.

01
WordsA word is a run of letters or digits. Apostrophes and hyphens inside a word keep it whole, so "don't" and "e-commerce" count once. Every word, stop words included, counts toward the total.
02
Phrases2–4 word phrases never cross sentence ends, commas, colons or line breaks. With stop-word filtering on, phrases can't start or end with a stop word, so "cost of coffee" counts but "of the" doesn't.
03
Target keywordsMatched as exact word sequences, ignoring case. Turn on plurals to count simple variants. HTML is reduced to visible text: scripts, styles and, optionally, the nav and footer are dropped.
04
PrivacyEverything runs in your browser. Pasted text and files are never uploaded. A URL fetch goes through a public page reader so the browser can read the page.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions

What is keyword density?

Keyword density is how often a word or phrase appears in a text, shown as a percentage of the total word count. A keyword used 10 times in a 1,000-word article has a density of 1%.

How is keyword density calculated?

The standard formula is (uses ÷ total words) × 100. Some tools use a word-share formula, where a multi-word phrase counts once per word it contains, so a 2-word phrase scores double. Pick either under Analysis settings. The panel always shows which one is in use.

What is a good keyword density for SEO?

Google publishes no ideal number. Most SEO writers keep a primary keyword between about 0.5% and 2.5% and treat anything above 3% as a warning sign. Long-tail phrases naturally sit lower. Treat the range as a guardrail, not a target to hit to two decimal places.

Does keyword density still matter in 2026?

Not as a ranking factor on its own. Search engines understand topics, synonyms and context. Density still catches two real problems quickly: a page that never clearly names what it's about, and a page that repeats a phrase until it reads as spam.

What is keyword stuffing, and how do I fix it?

Keyword stuffing means repeating a word or phrase unnaturally to try to rank, and Google's spam policies name it. To fix it, swap repeats for pronouns, synonyms or more specific terms. Cut the phrase from subheadings where it adds nothing, or add useful content so the same uses spread over more words.

How many times should I use my keyword?

Enter your keyword and the panel works it out from your word count and range. A rough rule is one use per 100–200 words, plus the title, H1, opening paragraph and URL slug.

Should stop words be counted?

They always count toward the total word count, so your percentages are honest. With filtering on, they're hidden from the 1-word list and phrases can't start or end with one. You see "cold brew coffee", not "the cold".

What's the difference between 1, 2, 3 and 4-word analysis?

One-word lists show which single terms dominate. Two- to four-word lists show the phrases you actually rank for, like "cold brew concentrate" or "how to make cold brew". Over-use usually shows up in the 2–3 word lists first.

Is my text stored or uploaded?

No. Pasted text and uploaded files are analysed by JavaScript inside your browser tab and never sent to a server. Your last draft is saved in your own browser's local storage so a refresh doesn't lose it. Clear removes it. Only the URL option requests a page, the one you name.

Can I check a live URL or a competitor's page?

Yes. Use "Fetch a URL" for any public page. Use the Compare tab to put a ranking page next to your draft. Some sites block automated readers. If a fetch fails, open the page, copy its text or HTML, and paste it instead.

Why do my numbers differ from another checker?

Checkers differ in three ways: whether stop words count toward the total, which formula they use, and whether phrases may cross punctuation. Our rules are listed under "Open method". Switch the formula to word share if you're matching a tool that uses it.

Is there a word limit or a daily limit?

No. No word cap, no daily check limit, no account. Very long texts are limited only by your device's memory.
