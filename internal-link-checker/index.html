Internal and external links

What the two kinds of links do

Internal links connect pages on your own site. They help readers find the next useful page, and they show search engines which pages matter and how they relate. A page with no internal links pointing to it is hard for anyone to find.

External links point to other sites. Linking to a good source helps readers and costs you nothing. Paid or untrusted links should carry rel="sponsored", rel="ugc" or rel="nofollow", and the checker shows which ones do.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions

What is an internal link?

An internal link points to another page on the same website, like a blog post linking to a product page. An external link points to a different website. Links to a subdomain (blog.example.com from example.com) are counted as external and marked "subdomain".

How many internal links should a page have?

There's no fixed number. Most well-linked articles have a handful of contextual internal links in the body text, each pointing to a page that genuinely helps the reader. Navigation and footer links come on top of that. Use the "Body text" filter to count only the ones in your content.

Why does the checker show more links than I added?

Menus, headers, footers and sidebars add links to every page. Switch the filter to "Body text" to see only the links inside your content.

Does this check whether links are broken?

No. It lists and classifies every link on the page and its anchor text. It doesn't visit each linked page, so it can't report status codes or redirects.

What counts as a generic anchor?

Anchors such as "click here", "read more", "learn more", "here" and "this link". They tell readers and search engines nothing about the page they lead to.

Is the page I check stored?

No. Pasted HTML is analysed in your browser. A URL is fetched through a public page reader so the browser can read it, then analysed in your browser. Some sites block automated readers; if a fetch fails, paste the page source instead.
